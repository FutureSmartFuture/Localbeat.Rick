# Ideal customer profile and verticals

Who buys local-events data via API, why, and the proof that lands with each. This is a reasoned draft. Confirm and reprioritize against your real pipeline.

## The shape of a good fit

A company whose product, content, or decisions get better with a live, structured feed of what is happening locally, and that would rather license a national corpus than build and maintain scrapers for 300K+ venues. They have engineers who can consume an API, a budget owner who cares about coverage and freshness, and a use case where "local events" is core, not decorative.

## Verticals, use cases, and the proof that closes

**Media and publishers (local news, city guides, "things to do" sites).**
Use: fill and freshen event listings and calendars without a manual team. Proof that lands: a live pull for their coverage city showing breadth and recurrence they cannot match by hand. Buyer cares about coverage and description fill rate.

**Tourism and destination marketing (DMOs, visitor bureaus, hospitality).**
Use: power "what's on" for visitors, feed concierge and booking surfaces. Proof: a themed pull (music, festivals, family) for their destination and a peak-season window. Buyer cares about category coverage and venue quality.

**Real estate and commercial real estate (portals, brokerages, CRE analytics).**
Use: neighborhood vibrancy and "life around this address" signals. Proof: event density by zip over time, tied to specific listings' locations. Buyer cares about geographic precision and consistency.

**Retail, out-of-home, and location analytics.**
Use: foot-traffic drivers and event-aware planning. Proof: event volume near their sites by day and category. Buyer cares about accuracy of venue coordinates and recurrence.

**AI companies and app builders needing grounded local data.**
Use: ground assistants and agents in real local events instead of stale web scrapes; training and retrieval data. Proof: freshness, structure, and the taxonomy. Buyer cares about API ergonomics, coverage, and licensing terms.

## Disqualifiers

No engineering capacity to consume an API and no appetite for a managed/consulting path; a one-city need too small to justify a license; a use case where public web search already suffices.
